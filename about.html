About Bihar

Geography

Bihar is a state located in the eastern part of India. It covers an area of approximately 94,163 square kilometers, making it the 12th largest state in the country by area. Bihar shares its borders with Uttar Pradesh to the west, Nepal to the north, West Bengal to the east, and Jharkhand to the south. The River Ganga divides Bihar into two nearly equal parts and serves as the lifeline of the state. Several major rivers including the Kosi, Gandak, Son, Bagmati, and Punpun flow through Bihar, making its land highly fertile and suitable for agriculture. The state lies in the Indo-Gangetic Plain, one of the most fertile regions in the world. Agriculture is the backbone of Bihar's economy, with rice, wheat, maize, sugarcane, and pulses being the major crops. Bihar experiences three main seasons: summer, monsoon, and winter. The climate is generally humid subtropical with hot summers and moderate winters.

Demographics

Bihar is one of the most populous states in India. According to recent surveys, the population of Bihar exceeds 130 million people. It has a high population density, reflecting its importance as a major cultural and economic region in eastern India. Hindi and Urdu are the official languages of the state, while Bhojpuri, Maithili, Magahi, Angika, and Bajjika are widely spoken in different regions. Bihar has a rich cultural diversity with people belonging to various communities and traditions. The literacy rate of Bihar has improved significantly over the last two decades due to various educational initiatives and government programs. The state has a large youth population, making it one of the youngest states in India. This demographic advantage provides significant opportunities for economic growth, innovation, and workforce development. Major cities of Bihar include Patna, Gaya, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Darbhanga, Purnia, and Bihar Sharif. Patna serves as the capital city and the primary center for administration, education, healthcare, and business activities.

History

Bihar has one of the richest historical legacies in India and is often referred to as the cradle of Indian civilization. The region was home to several ancient kingdoms including Magadha, Videha, Anga, and the Vajji Confederacy. The powerful Magadha Empire emerged in Bihar and later gave rise to the Maurya Empire under Emperor Chandragupta Maurya and Emperor Ashoka. Ashoka played a crucial role in spreading Buddhism across Asia after the Kalinga War. Bihar is also closely associated with the origins of Buddhism and Jainism. Lord Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, one of the most important pilgrimage sites in the world. Similarly, Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism, spent a significant part of his life in the region. The ancient Nalanda University, located in Bihar, was one of the world's first residential universities and attracted scholars from China, Korea, Tibet, and other parts of Asia. During medieval times, Bihar witnessed the rule of various dynasties including the Palas, Delhi Sultanate, Mughals, and later the British Empire. After India's independence in 1947, Bihar became an important state of the Indian Union. Today, Bihar continues to preserve its rich heritage while progressing in education, infrastructure, agriculture, technology, and tourism.

Tourism

Bihar is home to several world-famous tourist destinations. Bodh Gaya, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, attracts millions of Buddhist pilgrims every year. Nalanda University Ruins showcase the glorious educational heritage of ancient India. Other major attractions include Rajgir, Vaishali, Vikramshila, Kesaria Stupa, Golghar, Takht Sri Patna Sahib, Valmiki Tiger Reserve, and Kakolat Waterfall. These destinations reflect Bihar's unique blend of spirituality, history, culture, and natural beauty.
